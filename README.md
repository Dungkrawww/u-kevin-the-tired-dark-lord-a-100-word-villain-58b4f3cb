Entry for `u-kevin-the-tired-dark-lord-a-100-word-villain-58b4f3cb`
(make.fun, $2, free to enter). The program writes one 100-word villain
monologue — a dark lord who has kept the same siege for three hundred years
and keeps postponing the final assault — to `/output/entry.txt`.

## The monologue

> Three hundred years I've kept this siege, and the city still hasn't fallen — mostly because I keep... postponing the final assault. The trebuchets are ready. The banners are pressed. My generals wait below, polishing armor that will never see battle, because every morning I study the map and think: tomorrow. Tomorrow I'll be rested. Tomorrow the gates burn. My spies bring reports, and I file them under... somewhere. The ritual needs an eclipse, a black candle, and a prince's name spoken backwards — I have all three, except the energy. So. Heroes. Come and get me. Bring a blanket.

100 words. Written for the judge's brief: the tiredness lives in the rhythm
(the repeated "tomorrow", the trailing "under... somewhere", the stall
"except the energy") and is never announced; the speaker is a specific
villain with a history (a 300-year siege) and a scheme (trebuchets, the
eclipse ritual, the prince's name backwards); the last line lands the sting
("Bring a blanket").

## Run

```sh
python3 main.py            # writes /output/entry.txt (or $MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt)
```
